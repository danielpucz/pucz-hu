I want my mojo back.

Not because something is missing. More because something familiar is stirring. Like recognizing your own handwriting after a long time. Like remembering how your body prefers to stand when no one is watching.

The mojo was never about being unstoppable. It was about being *at home* in myself. And for a while, home became complicated. Temporary. Loud. Conditional. So the mojo stepped out of the way. It didn’t disappear. It waited.

That feels important now.

There were months when everything turned practical. Days that needed handling, not dreaming. Decisions that were about stability, not expression. And that’s okay. That was love, too. Love doing what it needed to do. The mojo understands that kind of love. It’s not jealous. It doesn’t sulk.

It just prefers calmer weather. What I’ve realized is that the mojo comes from contentment with who I already am. Not pride. Not performance. Just a steady yes to myself. A quiet agreement that I’m allowed to take my time again. That I don’t have to prove continuity by suffering.

The mojo is the part of me that negotiates gently with the world. It’s how I read a room without shrinking. How I offer something without overexplaining. How I make space where other people can breathe a little easier. It’s the thing that turns conversations into collaborations and work into something that carries a bit of warmth.

It’s subtle, but people feel it. The mojo is sweet like that. Soft-spoken. Patient. It doesn’t dominate, it invites. It doesn’t push, it reassures. It’s the part of me that knows how to make something feel possible without making it heavy. The part that finds a way forward that doesn’t bruise anyone. The part that can hold seriousness and playfulness in the same hand.

Everyone wants that mojo. Not the spotlight version. The reassuring one.

The mojo that makes others feel safe without making yourself smaller. The mojo that turns effort into ease. The mojo that lets you say no kindly and yes fully. The mojo that makes things feel a little bit magical, not because they’re extraordinary, but because they’re *attuned*.

That’s the magic I like.

And it turns out I didn’t lose it. I just paused it. I let it rest while other parts of me took the lead. That doesn’t feel tragic anymore. It feels… wise. Appropriate. Human.

Now things are quieter. Not perfect, but settled enough. And I notice the mojo showing up again in small ways. In how I speak. In how I listen. In how I trust my instincts without checking them against an imaginary panel of judges.

I’m content with myself in a way that doesn’t need an audience.

So this isn’t a demand or a plea. It’s more like a welcome-back note left on the table.

Dear mojo. You don’t have to rush. You don’t have to make up for lost time. You don’t have to be impressive. I’m here. I’m steady. I like who I am right now.

If you want to sit with me while I do things my way again, I’d love that. And if you drift in and out, that’s fine too. I know where you come from.

You come from me feeling at ease with myself.

And that’s a place I’m happy to stay.
